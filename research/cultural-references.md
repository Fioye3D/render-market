# Cultural references for the starter collection

**Reviewed:** 29 October 2026. This is a limited reference review for digital design content, not cultural-authenticity certification or a pan-African study. No source photographs or museum assets were redistributed. All listing imagery was generated from original scripted Blender geometry.

## Earthen courtyard

[UNESCO: Old Towns of Djenné](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/116/) describes the role of earthen construction and distinctive civic architecture in Djenné, Mali. The sample uses an original courtyard composition to explore earthen materiality. It does not reproduce a named building, mosque or sacred site, and must not be labelled as a measured Djenné reconstruction. Sahel-inspired is an interpretive design description, not evidence of architectural accuracy.

## Danfo / Lagos

[Lagos Metropolitan Area Transport Authority: Lagos Bus Reform Project report](https://www.lamata-ng.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Final-LBR-Project-Report.pdf) documents danfo services in Lagos. [Emeka Ogboh's gallery portfolio](https://imanefares.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/if-emekaogboh-portfolio-en-2025.pdf) discusses the yellow bus with black stripes as part of Lagos everyday life. The reference informs the subject and context; the original sample does not copy the artist's installation or use a vehicle manufacturer's marks.

## Bolgatanga basket reference

[Spurlock Museum, University of Illinois: basket 2013.05.1673](https://www.spurlock.illinois.edu/collections/search-collection/details.php?a=2013.05.1673) records a grass basket from Bolgatanga, Ghana. The digital basket is an original open woven-form study. It is not a replica of the accessioned object or an artisan-authenticated Bolga product.

## Aṣọ òkè reference

[V&A: Cloth of a continent](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/cloth-of-a-continent-africa-fashion) discusses Yoruba strip-woven aṣọ òkè from south-western Nigeria. [Smithsonian: Man's garments](https://www.si.edu/object/mans-garments%3Anmafa_2000-18-1) records Yoruba cloth and its evolving colour/stripe treatments. The procedural striped fabric is a new interpretation; it does not copy a historic pattern or claim traditional weaving authenticity. The UI uses an unaccented search-friendly display title; full naming/context is retained in this documentation.

## Gangan reference

[Hartenberger World Musical Instrument Collection: Yoruba Dundun/Gangan](https://wmic.net/yoruba-dundun-gangan-ah/) records hourglass-shaped, double-headed pressure drums associated with Yoruba communities in Nigeria/Benin. [University of Florida Center for African Studies](https://africa.ufl.edu/ede-yan-the-language-of-yan-in-yoruba-art-and-ritual-of-egungun/) describes language, drumming and cultural context. The original model studies an hourglass body, two membranes, tension cords and a curved beater. It is not an ethnographic replica, ritual object, acoustic simulation or animated instrument.

## Representation boundary

This collection is West Africa focused. It should later broaden through references and collaboration with creators from other African regions, including contemporary urban environments and everyday objects. Avoid replacing specific identities with generic “tribal” descriptions, treating cultural forms as interchangeable, or inferring authenticity from a creator's location.

## Expanded catalogue references

- Djembe: [The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Jembe](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/503286) documents the goblet-drum form in the Mandé region. The digital study is an original interpretation, not a replica of that instrument.
- Coastal doorway: [UNESCO, Stone Town of Zanzibar](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/173/) and [the Met's carved-door record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/930571) provide architectural context. The sample uses a new geometric panel composition; no historic carving or calligraphy is copied.
- Kente: [V&A, Cloth of a continent](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/cloth-of-a-continent-africa-fashion) describes strip-woven cloth associated with Asante and Ewe communities. The new procedural colour study is not labelled as a traditional authenticated design.
- Calabash bowls: [Smithsonian National Museum of African Art](https://africa.si.edu/collection/object/nmafa_2000-29-27) records a gourd bowl from Gombe, Nigeria. The sample set uses plain original vessel geometry; that object's decoration is not reproduced.
- Baobab: [Kew, Adansonia digitata](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/558628-1) documents a range across tropical/southern Africa and part of the Arabian Peninsula. The stylised dry-season model is not a botanical scan or an exclusively African-species claim.
- Jiko: [Kenya's REREC stove information](https://rerec.co.ke/improved-cook-stoves-jikos-2/) provides contemporary context. The study is a visual prop, not a functional stove specification.

The Lagos kiosk, natural-fibre mat, market handcart and earthenware jar are original scene studies with proposed African settings. They are not authenticated community artefacts. Sanctuary Temple is a separate fantasy portfolio scene; no regional cultural origin is asserted.
